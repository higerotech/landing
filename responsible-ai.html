Responsible AI Policy

Effective from: 16 September 2026 Version: 1.0 Scope: Higerotech and its projects

The two commitments that sum up this document: we do not use a client's data to train, fine-tune or develop models — and that prohibition extends to anonymised or aggregated derivatives — and every output of an AI system is support for a human decision, never the decision itself.

1. Principles

Higerotech presents itself as an AI-First consultancy. That label carries an obligation to state precisely what we do with artificial intelligence and, above all, what we do not do with the data entrusted to us.

The principles we apply: human oversight of every output with effects; minimisation — the model receives the minimum data necessary; traceability of what produced each result; transparency about where an automated system is involved; and accountability, in the concrete sense that there is always an identifiable person answering for the decision.

We adopt them as a voluntary framework. We do not claim compliance with any AI regulation: the European AI Act does not apply to us today, and in Venezuela the national instrument on artificial intelligence does not have the force of an enacted statute. Saying otherwise would be convenient and false.

2. Where we use AI

In two distinct places, which should not be conflated:

  • In our own development process, as an assistive tool for writing, reviewing and documenting code. The result goes through human review and the same automated checks as any other change.
  • In the systems we build for clients, where the project calls for it: process automation, document classification, anomaly detection, transaction profiling models. There, AI is part of the product, and its governance is agreed in the project contract.

3. What we do not do with a client's data

We do not use a client's data to train, fine-tune, evaluate or develop artificial intelligence models, whether our own or third parties'. The prohibition extends to the data in any derived form: anonymised, pseudonymised or aggregated sets, synthetic data derived from real data, and vector representations.

Closing the loop on derivatives is not rhetorical. Without it, "we anonymise it and train on that" would be compatible with the letter of the commitment, and that is exactly the practice the commitment exists to prevent.

Nor do we put a client's production data into third-party AI tools. Where work on real data is necessary, it is done in the client's environment, with named access and activity logging; development and test environments use synthetic or irreversibly anonymised data.

That last point is also why an equivalent clause exists in our data processing agreement: in Venezuela, the misappropriation and improper disclosure of personal data are criminal offences carrying prison sentences, and the typical conduct is precisely copying production data to a laptop or pasting it into an outside tool. The rule protects the client and protects whoever holds the credentials.

4. Mandatory human review

The outputs of the AI systems we deliver are advisory and non-binding. The client retains the final decision and undertakes not to act on them without human review where the decision has legal or economic effects on a person: declining a transaction, blocking an account, rejecting an application, classifying someone as a risk.

We design systems so that such review is genuinely possible: with the data in view, the reason for the result, and traceability of who decided what and when. A human review that can only press "approve" is not a review.

5. Profiling and AML/CFT models

We build models that profile transactions for anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist-financing purposes. The allocation of responsibilities is explicit, because that is where regulatory risk concentrates:

ResponsibilityWhose
Building the engine, documenting it and making it auditableHigerotech
Setting thresholds, rules and risk parametersThe client
Deciding on each alert and acting on itThe client
Reporting to the supervisor and answering its requestsThe client
Explaining the decision to the affected personThe client
Obtaining any authorisation or no-objection from the supervisorThe client

The reason is legal and not negotiable: the party bound by prevention regulation is the regulated institution, not its technology provider. Higerotech is not authorised as a banking-sector institution and does not provide financial services on its own account, and it does not guarantee regulatory approval of any development. Anyone promising otherwise is selling something they cannot deliver.

6. Traceability

The AI systems we deliver record which input produced which output, with which version of the model or rules, and at what time. Without that there is no possible audit, no way to explain a decision six months later, and no way to demonstrate that the system did what it said it did.

7. Model providers

Where a development uses third-party models, we select only providers whose contractual terms exclude the use of inputs for training, and we declare them as sub-processors in the project's data processing agreement, with their purpose and location.

We verify that exclusion before promising it. A no-training commitment that depends on a tool which does train on what it receives is not a commitment: it is a breach with good intentions.

8. Limitations and what we do not guarantee

  • We do not guarantee results. Neither accuracy, nor completeness, nor that a model will detect every case it should. Statistical systems err in both directions: false positives and false negatives.
  • We do not guarantee the absence of bias. We work to detect and reduce it, with the data and metrics the project allows, and we document it. That is not the same as eliminating it.
  • Models decay. Their quality depends on data that changes; without monitoring and retraining, they degrade. If the client does not contract that maintenance, the degradation is foreseeable and is not a defect in the deliverable.
  • We hold no AI certifications and will not claim any until we do.
